# Returns record checks

Checked 4 November 2026. These are evidence prompts and conservative internal controls, not a legal certification. The operator determines the applicable law, defect severity and remedy. Business-to-business sales, cross-border transactions, service contracts and negotiated exceptions need a scoped policy before use.

## Australia: consumer guarantees

The ACCC distinguishes major and minor problems and says store policies cannot remove statutory rights. A major product problem can give the consumer the choice of refund or replacement. Change-of-mind returns are a different policy question. Source: [ACCC repair, replace, refund, cancel](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/problem-with-a-product-or-service-you-bought/repair-replace-refund-cancel).

The desk records basis, severity, customer choice and evidence. REVIEW-ASSESSMENT and REVIEW-SEVERITY identify missing review. AU-NZ-CHOICE identifies an unrecorded choice for a major defect. AU-NZ-REMEDY compares a recorded major-defect remedy with that choice. The CLI requires a matching refund or exchange for this simplified major-defect workflow. Other negotiated outcomes need a reviewed policy change. It never determines severity from a return reason or applies a store return window to remove rights.

## New Zealand: faulty and unsafe goods

Consumer Protection explains the CGA protections for faulty and unsafe products and the remedies depending on the fault. Source: [Faulty and unsafe products](https://www.consumerprotection.govt.nz/general-help/common-consumer-issues/faulty-and-unsafe-products).

The same evidence checks apply to NZ cases. SAFETY-HOLD identifies recorded unsafe stock. The internal handling control forces that stock into quarantine and refuses restocking damaged or unsafe items. It is not a complete product-safety or recall procedure. Inspection and physical receipt requirements are internal controls, not a claim that law always requires a returned item before a remedy. REVIEW-JURISDICTION flags unknown country; country is explicitly supplied and represents the scoped review jurisdiction, not proof the law applies.

## Privacy retention

NZ-IPP9 flags cases past their review date. [Privacy Commissioner Principle 9](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/9/) requires not retaining personal information longer than needed for a lawful purpose. The importer sets a 365-day review date, not a statutory retention term or deletion deadline. The business must decide retention, legal holds, access and secure disposal, including copies of original imports, reports and backups. This base never deletes automatically.

## Targets and limits

The seven-day import service target is a configurable demonstration policy. No legal deadline is inferred from it. Completion needs an external reference supplied by an operator; it does not verify the external payment or shipment. Currency is supplied for each import because the documented payload does not establish a currency field for the amounts this importer uses. This system records evidence and checks missing fields. It does not certify that the facts recorded are true.
